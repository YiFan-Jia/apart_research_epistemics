## 4. Who is judging

- **No commercial sponsor is listed.** Apart runs it. Alex Csaky (Epistack Labs; co-author of the "State of the Field: AI for Epistemics and Coordination" report) co-designed the tracks and is a judge.
- **Forethought is the intellectual centre.** Owen Cotton-Barratt is a speaker and most of the reading list is Forethought's. Work framed as "helping people decide well" fits better than work framed only as a model defect.
- **23 judges, mostly non-clinical:** forecasting (FutureSearch), policy (ALTER, Arcadia, Pitt Cyber, Princeton CITP), philosophy and decision theory (UC Irvine), economics, UK AISI engineering, LLM evaluation engineers. One has a clinical-trial background (Bruno Balen).
- **Implication:** medicine works as a test bed, but the finding has to matter to someone who has never seen a patient.

## 5. What has won at Apart

Evidence: three recent first-place projects with reviewer comments, and the full results of the Jan 2026 AI Manipulation Hackathon (532 sign-ups, 70 submissions).

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| A narrow question with a measurable outcome | Reviewers praised claims that stayed inside the evidence |
| Controls and baselines | The Digital Minds winner was praised for a factorial design, held-out conditions and lexical baselines |
| Several models, quantitative results | The Manipulation winner used 12 models and 22 scenarios in one study, 153 scenarios in another |
| A validated judge | That winner checked its LLM judge against human raters (r = 0.83) |
| A surprising, quotable finding | "0% explicit lies, but strategic evasion" was the line a reviewer picked out |
| Honest negative results | A non-significant secondary result was reported as such and reviewers liked it |

**What reviewers ask for:** significance tests, more detail on the dataset, replication beyond one model.

**Crowding:**
- About 17 of the 70 Manipulation submissions were on sycophancy.
- Two were medical sycophancy ("The Alignment Gap", "Dangerous Affirmation or Responsible Correction?") and neither placed.
- The sycophancy entry that did place (3rd) had a clean new axis: three languages (English, Japanese, Bengali). Alex Csaky co-authored it.

**Field size (estimate):** if the January ratio of submissions to sign-ups holds, roughly 130–150 submissions.
